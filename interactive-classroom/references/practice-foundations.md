# Practice Design 

## Default minimum for mastery-loop courses

For every core inventory concept:
- **2 worked examples**: ideally one standard and one contrast/boundary example;
- **3 practice items**: guided, independent, and transfer/retrieval;
- one explain-back opportunity;
- objective coverage again in the final mastery gate.

## Worked-example fading

Do not jump directly from a full solution to a hard independent item. A useful sequence is:

1. Worked: full reasoning shown.
2. Completion: learner supplies the last step or missing reason.
3. Guided: learner solves with a hint ladder available.
4. Independent: no hint until an actual attempt.
5. Transfer: changed representation/context.

## Hint ladder

Recommended maximum 3 levels:
1. **Point** to the relevant variable/condition/diagram relation.
2. **Teach** the principle but not the concrete step.
3. **Bottom out** with the key step/answer, then require a why/explain-back.

Avoid a hint that is simply the answer in different words.

## Error analysis

For formal/quantitative subjects, include some tasks that ask for the **first invalid step** in a plausible wrong solution. This tests structural understanding better than repeated substitution.

## Cumulative retrieval

After 2–3 new concepts, add a short quiz mixing at least two objective IDs. The learner should have to decide *which* principle applies before calculating.

## Quantity over redundancy

More practice does not mean dozens of isomorphic questions. Prefer 3–6 varied items per core concept over 15 copies with changed numbers.
